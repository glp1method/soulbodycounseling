Clinical supervision for LPCC and LAC candidates

You finished school, and now you need someone to sign your hours. I'd like to be more useful to you than that.

How I Supervise

Supervision with me is practical. We work your real cases, I give you straight feedback, and we cover the parts school skipped: documentation, boundaries, and how to pace yourself so you're still in this field in five years.

Hours & Paperwork

What Colorado asks of you, and what I handle.

◎

LPCC candidates

2,000 hours over at least 24 months, with 50 hours of supervision for every 1,000 hours of practice. At least half of that supervision has to be individual.

⟡

LAC candidates

2,000 hours of clinically supervised addiction work experience, supervised by an LAC or another qualified supervisor.

I keep a record of every hour and sign your verified statements on the board's forms, so you're not chasing me at the end.

Common Questions

Questions people ask first

Can you sign LAC hours?

Yes. I hold an active Colorado LAC, so I can supervise LAC candidates toward licensure.

Do you supervise by video?

Yes. Video supervision counts toward Colorado hours, and you can mix video and in-person sessions.

Can I do group to lower the cost?

Yes. Groups run 2 to 4 people at $90 each, and for LPC candidates, Colorado lets up to half of your required supervision happen in a group.

What's the first step?

A free 15-minute call. We'll talk about where you are in your hours, what your caseload looks like, and whether we're a fit.
